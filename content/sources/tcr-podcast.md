## Pearls extracted from deep-reads (use inline-cited)

### Aortic dissection (S2E5)

- Mortality rises ~1–2% **per hour** untreated.
- 20% have normal CXR. 6.3% are painless.
- Avoid anticoagulation if AD suspected (troponin can mislead toward ACS).
- BP target: systolic <120 mmHg with labetalol (0.25 mg/kg bolus).
- Always pair alpha/beta blocker with nitrate to avoid peripheral
  vasoconstriction reflex.
- Type A → surgical. Type B → medical (in most cases).
- CTA is gold standard.

### Posterior circulation stroke (S2E3)

- Define dizziness precisely: vertigo vs presyncope.
- **FAST misses up to 50% of posterior strokes.**
- NIHSS underestimates posterior strokes (headache, vertigo, truncal ataxia).
- HINTS = Head Impulse + Nystagmus + Test of Skew (acute vestibular syndrome).

### Paeds seizures (S2E9)

- Status definition: continuous activity >5 min OR sequential seizures
  without consciousness recovery.
- Red flags: head injury, developmental regression, preceding headache,
  bleeding diathesis / anticoagulation, focal signs.
- Neonatal status: phenobarbitone 20 mg/kg IV loading; phenytoin
  20 mg/kg slow IV; levetiracetam 40 mg/kg.

### Delirium (S1E19)

- 4AT screen for all older patients (no training needed).
- Three subtypes — hyperactive, hypoactive, mixed. Hypoactive easily
  missed and more severe.
- Get collateral on baseline cognition + onset.
- Urine dipstick: positive results common in older adults without UTI
  symptoms — interpret in context.
- Haloperidol 0.5–1 mg first-line (monitor QT). Lorazepam if PD/LBD.

### TCA overdose (S1E2)

- QRS >100 ms = cardiac toxicity signal; >160 ms = 50% VT/VF risk.
- Sodium bicarb 1–2 mEq/kg bolus; infusion 150 mEq in 1 L D5 at
  250 mL/h. Goal pH 7.5–7.55.
- Seizures + agitation: benzodiazepines.
- Hypotension: crystalloid then noradrenaline.

### Upper GI bleed (S1E3)

- Glasgow-Blatchford <1 → safe for outpatient management
  (avoids 19–24% of admissions).
- Restrictive Hb target 7–8 g/dL unless haemodynamically unstable.
- Variceal: terlipressin + ceftriaxone prophylaxis (reduces all-cause
  mortality and rebleeding).
- High-dose IV PPI: 80 mg bolus then 8 mg/h.

### Chest trauma (S5E9)

- Silver trauma masks severity — older patients don't mount typical
  responses; CXR misses flail in 2D.
- DOAC-on-board chest trauma: discuss reversal with haematology;
  timing of last dose is key.
- POCUS: not a rule-out, but accelerates triage.
- Pain: nerve blocks (serratus anterior plane — SABRE trial).

### Hypertensive emergency (S1E13)

- Urgency = elevated BP, no end-organ damage.
- Emergency = BP elevation + end-organ damage (encephalopathy, ICH,
  MI, aortic dissection, AHF, eclampsia, AKI).
- Labetalol first-line; GTN for rapid onset/offset.
- Avoid amlodipine (6–12 h onset → dose stacking).
- Reduce 10–20% in the first hour.

### VTE / PE (S3E1)

- Wells low + PERC negative → no D-dimer needed (<2% probability).
- Age-adjusted D-dimer (ESC 2019) improves specificity in elderly.
- Massive PE + RV dysfunction → thrombolysis; intubation risks
  haemodynamic collapse.

### Perimortem caesarean (S1E7)

- Initiate within 5 min of arrest in women ≥20 weeks gestation.
- Manual left uterine displacement during CPR.
- Compressions 2–3 cm higher on sternum.
- IV access above the diaphragm.
- WOMAN trial: 1 g IV TXA post-delivery reduces bleeding.
