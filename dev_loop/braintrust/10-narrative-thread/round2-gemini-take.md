### 1. The Recurrence Audit is Mandatory
Opus’s Round 1 proposal to build continuity out of the existing patient roster was a mistake because it treated a content-economy artifact as a feature. The author is right: real Emergency Medicine does not look like a village clinic where you see the same six people every week. It is a river of strangers, punctuated by a tiny, highly specific cohort of frequent flyers.

Before we write a single line of narrative, we must audit the recurrence. Strip Beth, Williams, Brennan, Chloe, and Patel down to single appearances. Keep Stan. Stan is your safety-net frequent flyer. By culling the accidental repeats, we restore clinical realism and clear the stage for the actual narrative thread: the staff.

### 2. The NTS Thread is Pure Pedagogy
The author’s proposal to use an NTS/Human Factors narrative is brilliant because it solves the "soap opera" problem. If the staff drama is just who is sleeping with whom, it’s entertainment in a lab coat—distracting and irrelevant. But if the staff drama is a slow-motion demonstration of James Reason’s Swiss Cheese model, it is highly testable FRCEM curriculum.

The Royal College cares deeply about Crew Resource Management, situational awareness, and communication failures. A narrative thread that tracks a junior doctor (Akin) slowly degrading due to burnout, or a growing communication friction between the nursing coordinator and the consultant (McGrath), is pedagogically bulletproof. It starts innocuous in Shift 1 (a missing handover sheet, a sarcastic comment about bed management) and reaches a crescendo in Shift 8 or 9 (a critical incident where the player must catch a clinical error caused by a handover failure). This teaches the player to look for systemic red flags, not just physiological ones.

### 3. The Comedy Lives in the Margins
Round 1 dismissed entertainment because it assumed comedy would dilute the clinical cases. This is a failure of imagination. *Disco Elysium*’s comedy doesn't interrupt the detective work; it is the texture of the detective work. Emergency Medicine possesses a rich, dark, gallows humor that is essential for survival. 

We do not need to build a dedicated "Doctors' Mess" surface for slice-of-life beats. That is scope creep and breaks the time-pressure loop. Instead, the comedy must live in the margins of the existing surfaces:

*   **The Triage Notes (Vignette Intros):** Real triage notes are inherently absurd. ("Patient states a ghost punched him in the face. HR 110. EWS 2.") Let the first sentence of a vignette provide a micro-beat of bizarre ED reality before dropping into the serious clinical data.
*   **The Pre-Shift Handover:** This is where the NTS thread and the comedy fuse. Akin’s handover notes shouldn't just be clinical; they should be laced with the dry, exhausted banter of a junior doctor running on black coffee and spite. 
*   **The Post-Shift Memo:** McGrath’s feedback should read like a passive-aggressive bureaucratic masterpiece. 

### 4. The Two-Track Composition: Integrated, Not Parallel
The author asks if the heavy NTS narrative and the light comic relief balance each other out. They do, but only if they are spoken by the same characters. Do not create a "funny character" and a "serious character." 

The comedy *is* the symptom of the NTS strain. When Akin makes a dark joke in Shift 2 about the printer being broken and the nurses hiding the good ECG leads, it’s comic relief. When the same broken printer and hidden ECG leads cause a 15-minute delay in diagnosing a STEMI in Shift 8, it’s a catastrophic Human Factors failure. The lightness sets up the heaviness. This dual-register is exactly how *Disco Elysium* operates, and the Skitt's NPC roster (Akin, McGrath, the unseen nursing staff) is perfectly capable of carrying it.

### 5. The Execution
We abandon the idea of patient-based continuity (except for Stan) and commit entirely to a meta-layer staff narrative that teaches NTS through the lens of ED gallows humor. It requires zero new UI surfaces. It only requires a tonal rewrite of the Handover and Memo strings, and a ruthless culling of the accidental patient repeats.
